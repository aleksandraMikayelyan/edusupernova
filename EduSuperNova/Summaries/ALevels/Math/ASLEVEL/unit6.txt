UNIT 6: SERIES

INTRODUCTION TO SERIES
A series is formed by the sum of the terms of a sequence. This unit explores how to expand algebraic expressions using the binomial theorem and how to analyze arithmetic and geometric progressions.

SEQUENCE: An ordered list of numbers generated by a specific rule.
SERIES: The sum of the terms of a sequence.

THE BINOMIAL THEOREM
The binomial theorem provides a formula for expanding brackets containing two terms raised to a positive integer power n.

- The general expansion of (a + b)^n starts with a^n and ends with b^n.
- The coefficients of the expansion can be found using Pascal’s triangle.
- The specific term (n r) can be calculated using the formula: n! / [r!(n - r)!].

PASCAL'S TRIANGLE: A triangular array of numbers where each number is the sum of the two numbers directly above it, used to find binomial coefficients.

ARITHMETIC PROGRESSIONS
An arithmetic progression (A.P.) is a sequence in which each term is obtained by adding a constant value to the previous term.

DEFINITION: The constant value added to each term in an A.P. is known as the common difference (d).

- The general term (nth term) of an A.P. is: un = a + (n - 1)d.
- The sum of the first n terms (Sn) is given by: Sn = 0.5 * n * [2a + (n - 1)d].

GEOMETRIC PROGRESSIONS
A geometric progression (G.P.) is a sequence in which each term is obtained by multiplying the previous term by a fixed, non-zero number.

DEFINITION: The fixed number used to find the next term in a G.P. is called the common ratio (r).

- The general term (nth term) of a G.P. is: un = a * r^(n-1).
- The sum of the first n terms (Sn) is given by: Sn = a * (1 - r^n) / (1 - r).

SUM TO INFINITY
For some geometric series, the sum of terms approaches a finite limit as the number of terms increases indefinitely.

CONDITION: A sum to infinity (S∞) only exists if the absolute value of the common ratio is less than 1 (|r| < 1).

NOTE: The formula for the sum to infinity is: S∞ = a / (1 - r).

CONVERGENCE AND DIVERGENCE
Sequences and series can be classified based on their behavior as the number of terms increases.

- A sequence converges if its terms approach a specific finite value.
- A sequence diverges if its terms grow without bound or do not settle at a single value.
