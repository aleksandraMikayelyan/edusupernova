GOVERNMENT MICROECONOMIC INTERVENTION

    REASONS FOR GOVERNMENT INTERVENTION: MARKET FAILURE

    DEFINITION OF MARKET FAILURE

    Market failure occurs when the free market mechanism fails to allocate
    resources efficiently, leading to a loss of social welfare (Net
    Welfare Loss).

    MAIN CATEGORIES OF MARKET FAILURE
    * EXTERNALITIES: Spillovers to third parties not involved in the
      transaction.
    * PUBLIC GOODS: Goods that are non-excludable and non-rival.
    * MONOPOLY POWER: Firms with too much power charge high prices and
      under-produce.
    * ASYMMETRIC INFORMATION: When one party (usually the seller) knows
      more than the other, leading to poor choices.

   EXTERNALITIES (The "Spillover" Effects)
    - NEGATIVE EXTERNALITIES (External Costs): Costs imposed on third
      parties (e.g., pollution, passive smoking).
      * RESULT: Market OVER-PRODUCES these goods.
      * POLICY: Indirect taxes, regulations.
    - POSITIVE EXTERNALITIES (External Benefits): Benefits enjoyed by
      third parties (e.g., vaccination, education).
      * RESULT: Market UNDER-PRODUCES these goods.
      * POLICY: Subsidies, state provision.

    PUBLIC GOODS & THE FREE-RIDER PROBLEM
    Because public goods (e.g., national defense) are non-excludable,
    people can use them without paying.
    - RESULT: Private firms have no profit incentive to produce them.
    - MARKET FAILURE: The good is not produced at all (Missing Market).
    - SOLUTION: Provided by the government and funded through taxation.

    ASYMMETRIC INFORMATION & MERIT/DEMERIT GOODS
    Market failure often happens because consumers lack "Perfect
    Information."
    - MERIT GOODS: Better for the consumer than they realize (e.g.,
      health checkups). Under-consumed due to information failure.
    - DEMERIT GOODS: Worse for the consumer than they realize (e.g.,
      sugary drinks, junk food). Over-consumed due to information failure.

    CASE STUDY: SUGAR TAX (UK EXAMPLE)
        - THE PROBLEM: Overconsumption of sugar leads to obesity/diabetes
          (Negative Externality: costs to healthcare system) and information
          failure (consumers underestimate health risks).
        - THE INTERVENTION: A "Tiered Tax" on manufacturers.
        - THE OUTCOME: Encourages firms to reformulate (reduce sugar) to
          avoid the tax.


    METHODS AND EFFECTS OF GOVERNMENT INTERVENTION IN MARKETS

        Governments intervene to correct market failures (such as externalities
        or information gaps) and to achieve social equity.

        PRICE CONTROLS

        Legal limits placed on the market price to protect specific groups.

        A. PRICE CEILING (Maximum Price)
           - DEFINITION: A legal maximum price set BELOW the equilibrium.
           - PURPOSE: To ensure essential goods (rent, food) stay affordable.
           - IMPACTS:
             * Shortage: Quantity Demanded (Qd) > Quantity Supplied (Qs).
             * Non-Price Rationing: Use of queues, coupons, or seller bias.
             * Black Markets: Illegal sales above the legal price limit.
             * Quality Decline: Producers cut costs (e.g., poor maintenance).



        B. PRICE FLOOR (Minimum Price)
           - DEFINITION: A legal minimum price set ABOVE the equilibrium.
           - PURPOSE: To protect producer income (farmers) or workers (Min Wage).
           - IMPACTS:
             * Surplus: Quantity Supplied (Qs) > Quantity Demanded (Qd).
             * Inefficiency: Government may need to buy/store excess stock.
             * Demerit Goods: High floors can be used to reduce consumption
               of harmful products.

        TAXATION

        Used to fund public services and discourage "demerit" goods.

        - DIRECT TAXES: Paid from income/profits (e.g., Personal Income Tax).
        - INDIRECT TAXES: Imposed on spending (e.g., VAT, Excise Duties).
          * MECHANISM: Increases production costs; shifts Supply Curve LEFT.
          * TAX INCIDENCE: How the burden is shared based on elasticity.
            - Inelastic Demand: Consumer pays the majority of the tax.
            - Elastic Demand: Producer absorbs the majority of the tax.

        SUBSIDIES

        A government payment to producers to lower their costs of production.

        - MECHANISM: Lowers production costs; shifts Supply Curve RIGHT.
        - PURPOSES:
          * Support low-income producers (farmers).
          * Encourage "Merit Goods" (vaccines, education).
          * Support infant or eco-friendly industries (EVs, solar).
        - DRAWBACKS:
          * High Opportunity Cost: Uses money that could go to other services.
          * Inefficiency: Firms may stop innovating if they rely on aid.
          * Overconsumption: Can lead to environmental damage (e.g., energy).

    INCOME AND WEALTH INEQUALITY

    DEFINITIONS: INCOME VS. WEALTH
        - INCOME: A FLOW concept. The money received over a period of time
          (wages, interest, dividends, rent, transfer payments).
        - WEALTH: A STOCK concept. The value of assets owned at a specific
          point in time (savings, property, shares, pension funds).
        * LINK: High wealth generates more income (rent/interest); high
          income allows for more saving, which increases wealth.

    MEASURING INEQUALITY: THE GINI COEFFICIENT
        - The Gini Coefficient is a numerical measure of inequality.
        - SCALE: 0 to 1 (or 0% to 100%).
          * 0 = PERFECT EQUALITY (Everyone has the exact same income).
          * 1 = PERFECT INEQUALITY (One person has all the income).
        - AS LEVEL TIP: In exams, this is often linked to the LORENZ CURVE.
          The further the curve bows away from the 45-degree line, the
          higher the Gini coefficient.

    CAUSES OF INEQUALITY
        - HUMAN CAPITAL: Differences in education, skills, and qualifications.
        - HOUSEHOLD COMPOSITION: Single-parent vs. dual-income households.
        - WEALTH DISTRIBUTION: Inheritance (intergenerational inequality).
        - MARKET POWER: Monopoly power or discrimination in the labor market.
        - UNEMPLOYMENT: Long-term joblessness is a primary cause of poverty.

    THE EQUITY VS. EFFICIENCY DEBATE
        * ARGUMENTS FOR INEQUALITY (Growth Promoting):
          - INCENTIVES: High-income potential encourages people to work
            harder, gain skills, and take entrepreneurial risks.
        * ARGUMENTS AGAINST INEQUALITY (Socially Harmful):
          - SOCIAL FRICTION: Can lead to crime and political instability.
          - WASTED POTENTIAL: Talented people in poverty lack the means to
            get educated (Market Failure).
          - MARGINAL UTILITY: An extra $100 provides more benefit to a
            poor person than to a billionaire.

    GOVERNMENT INTERVENTION TOOLS
        Governments redistribute income using two main methods:

        FISCAL POLICY (TAXATION):
        - PROGRESSIVE TAXES: As income rises, the % of tax paid increases
          (e.g., Personal Income Tax). This reduces the "gap" between rich
          and poor.
        - REGRESSIVE TAXES: As income rises, the % of tax paid decreases
          (e.g., VAT/Sales tax, as it takes a larger % of a poor person's
          total income).

        TRANSFER PAYMENTS:
        - Payments made by the state to individuals without any exchange of
          goods or services (e.g., Pensions, Unemployment Benefits, Child
          Support).

        EVALUATION: DRAWBACKS OF REDISTRIBUTION
        - THE UNEMPLOYMENT TRAP: If benefits are too high, people have little
          incentive to find work.
        - THE BRAIN DRAIN: High progressive taxes may cause high-skilled
          workers to move to countries with lower taxes.
        - TAX EVASION: Extremely high taxes can lead to people hiding
          income in black markets or offshore accounts.
