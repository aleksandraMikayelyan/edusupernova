TOEFL ACADEMIC READING SUMMARY
TOPIC: THE EVOLUTION OF SYMBIOTIC RELATIONSHIPS

1. MAIN THESIS:
The passage discusses symbiosis as a fundamental biological interaction, categorized into three distinct types: mutualism, commensalism, and parasitism, each defined by the benefit or harm experienced by the participating organisms.

2. KEY POINTS:
- MUTUALISM: Both species benefit. The passage highlights the relationship between nitrogen-fixing bacteria and legumes, noting how it enhances soil fertility and plant growth.
- COMMENSALISM: One species benefits while the other is unaffected. An example provided is the cattle egret, which eats insects stirred up by grazing livestock without impacting the livestock's health.
- PARASITISM: One species (the parasite) benefits at the expense of the host. The text emphasizes that unlike predators, parasites usually do not kill their hosts immediately to ensure a long-term resource.

3. VOCABULARY & TERMINOLOGY:
- Obligate Symbiosis: Interactions where species are entirely dependent on each other for survival.
- Facultative Symbiosis: Interactions where species can survive independently but benefit from the association.
- Host: The larger organism in a symbiotic relationship.

4. SYNTHESIS FOR TOEFL WRITING/SPEAKING:
To summarize effectively for TOEFL, focus on the classification of these relationships. The author uses specific biological examples to illustrate how natural selection favors traits that improve an organism's chances of survival through these varied interactions.
