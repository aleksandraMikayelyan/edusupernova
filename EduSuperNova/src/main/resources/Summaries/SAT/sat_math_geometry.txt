GEOMETRY AND TRIGONOMETRY

This domain covers roughly 15% of SAT Math questions. Every question provides a reference sheet with key formulas — but knowing them cold saves precious time and lets you focus on problem-solving.

THE SAT REFERENCE SHEET (KNOW THESE)

Formulas given on the test:
- Area of circle: A = πr²
- Circumference of circle: C = 2πr
- Area of rectangle: A = lw
- Area of triangle: A = ½bh
- Pythagorean theorem: a² + b² = c²
- Special right triangles: 30-60-90 and 45-45-90
- Volume of rectangular prism: V = lwh
- Volume of cylinder: V = πr²h
- Volume of sphere: V = (4/3)πr³
- Volume of cone: V = (1/3)πr²h
- Volume of pyramid: V = (1/3)lwh

KEY CONCEPT: Even though these are given, you must know WHEN to apply each formula. Read each geometry problem twice: once to understand the shape, once to identify what's being asked (area, perimeter, volume, length, angle, etc.).

LINES AND ANGLES

COMPLEMENTARY ANGLES: Sum to 90°.

SUPPLEMENTARY ANGLES: Sum to 180°.

VERTICAL ANGLES: Formed by two intersecting lines. They are always equal.

CORRESPONDING ANGLES: Formed when a transversal crosses parallel lines. They are equal.

ALTERNATE INTERIOR ANGLES: On opposite sides of the transversal, between parallel lines. They are equal.

CO-INTERIOR (SAME-SIDE INTERIOR) ANGLES: On the same side of the transversal, between parallel lines. They are supplementary (sum to 180°).

REMEMBER: If you see parallel lines cut by a transversal on the SAT, mark all equal angles first (corresponding and alternate interior). This almost always reveals what you need.

TRIANGLES

ANGLE SUM: The interior angles of any triangle sum to 180°.

EXTERIOR ANGLE THEOREM: An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles.

TRIANGLE INEQUALITY: The sum of any two sides must be greater than the third side.

ISOSCELES TRIANGLE: Two equal sides and two equal base angles.

EQUILATERAL TRIANGLE: All sides equal, all angles equal 60°.

SIMILAR TRIANGLES: Same shape, different size. Corresponding angles are equal. Corresponding sides are proportional.

CONGRUENT TRIANGLES: Same shape AND size. All corresponding sides and angles are equal.

CRITERIA FOR SIMILAR TRIANGLES: AA (Angle-Angle) — if two angles are equal, the triangles are similar. This is the most-used criterion on the SAT.

KEY CONCEPT: Similar triangles appear constantly on the SAT, often embedded in larger figures. Look for shared angles or parallel lines creating equal angles. Once you identify similarity, set up a proportion with corresponding sides.

SPECIAL RIGHT TRIANGLES (MUST MEMORIZE):

45-45-90 TRIANGLE: Sides in ratio 1 : 1 : √2. If legs = x, hypotenuse = x√2. Example: legs of 5 → hypotenuse = 5√2.

30-60-90 TRIANGLE: Sides in ratio 1 : √3 : 2. Short leg = x, long leg = x√3, hypotenuse = 2x. Example: short leg of 4 → long leg = 4√3, hypotenuse = 8.

REMEMBER: The 30-60-90 triangle comes from cutting an equilateral triangle in half. The short leg is opposite the 30° angle, the long leg is opposite the 60° angle.

PYTHAGOREAN TRIPLES (useful for saving time): 3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17, 7-24-25. Their multiples work too: 6-8-10, 9-12-15, etc.

QUADRILATERALS AND POLYGONS

SUM OF INTERIOR ANGLES of a polygon with n sides: (n - 2) × 180°. For quadrilaterals: 360°. For pentagons: 540°. For hexagons: 720°.

PARALLELOGRAM: Opposite sides are parallel and equal; opposite angles are equal; diagonals bisect each other.

RECTANGLE: All properties of a parallelogram plus all right angles. Diagonals are equal in length.

RHOMBUS: All sides equal; diagonals bisect each other at right angles.

SQUARE: All properties of rectangle AND rhombus.

TRAPEZOID: One pair of parallel sides (the bases). Area = ½(b₁ + b₂)h.

CIRCLES

RADIUS: Distance from center to any point on the circle.

DIAMETER: Twice the radius. The longest chord.

CHORD: A line segment with both endpoints on the circle.

CENTRAL ANGLE: An angle whose vertex is at the center. It equals the arc it intercepts (in degrees).

INSCRIBED ANGLE: An angle whose vertex is on the circle. It equals HALF the arc it intercepts.

ARC LENGTH: (central angle / 360°) × 2πr.

SECTOR AREA: (central angle / 360°) × πr².

KEY CONCEPT: The inscribed angle theorem is crucial. An inscribed angle is always half the central angle that subtends the same arc. A special case: any inscribed angle that intercepts a semicircle (diameter) is always 90°.

TANGENT LINE: A line that touches a circle at exactly one point. A tangent is always perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of tangency.

CIRCLES IN THE COORDINATE PLANE

STANDARD FORM: (x - h)² + (y - k)² = r², where (h, k) is the center and r is the radius.

To find the equation of a circle: identify the center and radius, then substitute into standard form.

To convert from general form (x² + y² + Dx + Ey + F = 0): complete the square for both x and y terms.

IMPORTANT: If an SAT question gives a circle equation in general form, complete the square to identify the center and radius. Group x-terms and y-terms, complete each square, and rewrite.

COORDINATE GEOMETRY

DISTANCE FORMULA: d = √[(x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²]. This is just the Pythagorean theorem applied to coordinates.

MIDPOINT FORMULA: M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2).

PARALLEL LINES: Same slope, different y-intercepts.

PERPENDICULAR LINES: Slopes are negative reciprocals. If one slope is m, the other is -1/m. Their product is -1.

KEY CONCEPT: To find where two lines intersect, set their equations equal and solve. This is a system of two linear equations.

TRANSFORMATIONS

TRANSLATION: Slides the figure. (x, y) → (x + a, y + b).

REFLECTION over x-axis: (x, y) → (x, -y).

REFLECTION over y-axis: (x, y) → (-x, y).

REFLECTION over y = x: (x, y) → (y, x).

ROTATION 90° counterclockwise about origin: (x, y) → (-y, x).

ROTATION 180° about origin: (x, y) → (-x, -y).

DILATION: Scales the figure by factor k. (x, y) → (kx, ky). Distances multiply by |k|. Areas multiply by k².

NOTE: Under dilation, angle measures are preserved. Under reflection and rotation, both distances and angles are preserved (congruence transformations). These are called rigid motions.

TRIGONOMETRY

BASIC TRIG RATIOS (SOH-CAH-TOA):
- SIN: Opposite / Hypotenuse
- COS: Adjacent / Hypotenuse
- TAN: Opposite / Adjacent

REMEMBER: SOH-CAH-TOA only applies to RIGHT triangles. Identify which angle is the reference angle, then label the sides relative to it.

COMPLEMENTARY ANGLE RELATIONSHIP: sin(θ) = cos(90° - θ) and cos(θ) = sin(90° - θ). The SAT frequently tests this: sin(30°) = cos(60°), sin(x°) = cos(90° - x°).

PYTHAGOREAN IDENTITY: sin²(θ) + cos²(θ) = 1. This is used to find one trig ratio when given another.

KEY TRIG VALUES (MEMORIZE):
- sin 30° = cos 60° = 1/2
- sin 45° = cos 45° = √2/2
- sin 60° = cos 30° = √3/2
- tan 30° = √3/3 = 1/√3
- tan 45° = 1
- tan 60° = √3

USING TRIG TO FIND SIDES AND ANGLES:
- To find a side: identify the angle, set up the ratio, solve for the unknown
- To find an angle: use inverse trig (arcsin, arccos, arctan)

RADIANS AND DEGREES

RADIAN: An angle measurement based on arc length. One full revolution = 2π radians = 360°.

CONVERSION FORMULAS:
- Degrees to radians: multiply by π/180
- Radians to degrees: multiply by 180/π

KEY RADIAN VALUES: π/6 = 30°, π/4 = 45°, π/3 = 60°, π/2 = 90°, π = 180°, 2π = 360°.

ARC LENGTH using radians: s = rθ, where θ is in radians. This is simpler than the degree formula.

AREA OF SECTOR using radians: A = ½r²θ.

VOLUME AND SURFACE AREA

RECTANGULAR PRISM (BOX): Volume = lwh. Surface area = 2(lw + lh + wh).

CYLINDER: Volume = πr²h. Lateral surface area = 2πrh. Total surface area = 2πr² + 2πrh.

CONE: Volume = ⅓πr²h. Lateral surface area = πrl (l = slant height).

SPHERE: Volume = (4/3)πr³. Surface area = 4πr².

PYRAMID: Volume = ⅓(base area)h.

IMPORTANT: For composite solid problems, identify each component shape, calculate their volumes or areas separately, then add (for combined solids) or subtract (for hollowed-out solids).

TOP STRATEGIES FOR GEOMETRY

Strategy: Always draw and label a diagram if one is not provided. A sketch prevents orientation errors.

Strategy: Mark known angle measures and equal lengths directly on your diagram as you work through a problem.

Strategy: For missing angle problems, find ALL angles you can, even if the question asks for just one. The angle you need often becomes apparent.

Strategy: For similar triangle problems, always write the proportion with corresponding vertices aligned: triangle ABC ~ triangle DEF means AB/DE = BC/EF = AC/DF.

Strategy: For circles, the key is identifying what's being asked: arc length vs. sector area vs. chord length. Set up the fraction (central angle / 360°) first.

Strategy: When a geometry problem involves coordinates, use the distance formula, slope, or midpoint formula rather than visual estimation.

EXAMPLE: A right triangle has legs 6 and 8. What is the area of the circle inscribed in the triangle? Hypotenuse = 10 (3-4-5 triple × 2). For a right triangle, the inradius r = (a + b - c)/2 = (6 + 8 - 10)/2 = 2. Area = πr² = 4π.

COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Forgetting that a 30-60-90 triangle has the LONGER leg opposite the 60° angle (not the 30°)
- Using SOH-CAH-TOA with the wrong reference angle
- Mixing up arc length and sector area formulas
- Forgetting to halve the central angle to get the inscribed angle
- Using the diameter instead of the radius in circle formulas
- Not checking if triangles are similar before setting up a proportion (you must verify similarity first)
- Treating complementary and supplementary angles interchangeably
