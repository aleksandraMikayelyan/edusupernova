FURTHER MECHANICS

MOMENTUM AND IMPULSE
Linear momentum: p = mv (vector quantity; SI unit: kg m s⁻¹).
Newton's 2nd Law (impulse form): F = dp/dt; Impulse = Ft = Δ(mv) = mv - mu.
Conservation of linear momentum: In a closed system, total momentum is constant.
  m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂ (no external forces).
Elastic collision: Kinetic energy conserved; coefficient of restitution e = 1.
Inelastic collision: Kinetic energy not conserved; e < 1.
Perfectly inelastic: Objects stick together; maximum KE lost; e = 0.
Coefficient of restitution (Newton's Law of Restitution):
  e = (speed of separation) / (speed of approach) = (v₂ - v₁) / (u₁ - u₂).

WORK, ENERGY AND POWER
Work done: W = Fs cos θ (force × displacement × cos of angle between them).
Kinetic energy: KE = ½mv².
Gravitational potential energy: GPE = mgh.
Work-energy theorem: Net work done = change in KE.
Conservation of mechanical energy: KE + PE = constant (no friction/air resistance).
Power: P = dW/dt = Fv (rate of doing work; SI unit: watt W).
Efficiency: η = useful power output / total power input.

CIRCULAR MOTION
For uniform circular motion (constant speed, changing direction):
  Angular velocity: ω = dθ/dt = v/r (rad s⁻¹).
  Period: T = 2π/ω = 2πr/v.
  Centripetal acceleration: a = v²/r = ω²r (directed towards centre).
  Centripetal force: F = mv²/r = mω²r (not a new force; it is the net force towards centre).
Applications:
  Horizontal circle (e.g. car on banked road): Normal force and friction provide centripetal force.
  Conical pendulum: T cos θ = mg; T sin θ = mω²r → tan θ = ω²r/g.
  Vertical circle: At the top, minimum speed → mg = mv²/r → v = √(gr).
  At the bottom, N - mg = mv²/r.

HOOKE'S LAW AND ELASTIC STRINGS/SPRINGS
Hooke's Law: T = kx = λx/l (where λ is modulus of elasticity, l is natural length, x is extension).
Elastic potential energy (EPE): EPE = λx²/(2l) = kx²/2.
Energy conservation with elastic strings:
  KE + GPE + EPE = constant (if no friction).

SIMPLE HARMONIC MOTION (SHM)
Condition: Acceleration directed towards fixed point, proportional to displacement: a = -ω²x.
Displacement: x = A cos(ωt + φ); A = amplitude.
Velocity: v = ±ω√(A² - x²); maximum at x = 0: vₘₐₓ = ωA.
Period: T = 2π/ω (independent of amplitude for true SHM).
Simple pendulum: T = 2π√(l/g) for small angles.
Mass on spring: T = 2π√(m/k).

VARIABLE FORCES AND MOTION
When force varies with displacement: Use work-energy theorem W = ∫F dx.
When force varies with time: Use impulse-momentum F·t or ∫F dt = Δ(mv).
Resisted motion: Net force = driving force - resistance; use F = ma.
