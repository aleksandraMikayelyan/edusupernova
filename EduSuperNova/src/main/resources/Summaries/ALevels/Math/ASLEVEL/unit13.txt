NEWTON'S LAWS OF MOTION, ENERGY, WORK AND POWER
This unit extends mechanics to cover Newton's three laws of motion, connected particle problems, and the fundamental energy principles of work, energy, and power.

NEWTON'S FIRST LAW
An object remains at rest or continues moving at constant velocity unless acted upon by a resultant external force.

- A particle in equilibrium has zero resultant force.
- Constant velocity (not just rest) means zero resultant force.

NEWTON'S SECOND LAW
The resultant force on an object is directly proportional to its acceleration, in the direction of that acceleration.

DEFINITION: F = ma, where F is the resultant force in Newtons, m is the mass in kg, and a is the acceleration in m/s².

- Always apply F = ma in the direction of motion or the direction of acceleration.
- If multiple forces act, calculate the resultant first: ΣF = ma.

NEWTON'S THIRD LAW
For every action force, there is an equal and opposite reaction force acting on a different body.

- The two forces are equal in magnitude, opposite in direction, and act on different objects.
- This law is responsible for normal reaction forces and tension in connected strings.

CONNECTED PARTICLES
Problems involving two or more objects connected by a string (over a pulley or along a surface) are solved as a system.

Method:
1. Draw force diagrams for each particle separately.
2. Apply F = ma to each particle, using the same acceleration a if the string is inextensible.
3. Solve the simultaneous equations for a and the tension T.

WORK
Work is done when a force causes a displacement in the direction of the force.

DEFINITION: W = Fd cos θ, where F is the applied force, d is the displacement, and θ is the angle between the force and the direction of motion.

- Work is a scalar quantity, measured in Joules (J).
- If the force and displacement are in the same direction: W = Fd.
- No work is done if the force is perpendicular to the displacement (e.g., normal reaction on a horizontal surface).

ENERGY
Energy is the capacity to do work. It is measured in Joules (J) and is a scalar quantity.

KINETIC ENERGY (KE): The energy an object possesses due to its motion.
- KE = 0.5 × m × v²

GRAVITATIONAL POTENTIAL ENERGY (GPE): The energy stored due to an object's height above a reference level.
- GPE = mgh, where h is the height above the reference point.

THE WORK–ENERGY THEOREM
The net work done on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy.

Work done by resultant force = ΔKE = 0.5mv² - 0.5mu²

CONSERVATION OF MECHANICAL ENERGY
When only gravity does work (no friction), the total mechanical energy is conserved.

KE + GPE = constant, so: 0.5mv² + mgh = 0.5mu² + mgh₀

POWER
Power is the rate at which work is done.

DEFINITION: P = W / t = Fv, where F is the driving force and v is the velocity.

- Power is measured in Watts (W), where 1 W = 1 J/s.
- For a vehicle moving at constant velocity, the driving force equals the resistance force, and P = Fv gives the engine's power output.

NOTE: When friction or resistance is present, energy is not conserved. Calculate the work done against friction and subtract it from the energy equation.
