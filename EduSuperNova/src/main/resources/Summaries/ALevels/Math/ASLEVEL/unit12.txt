INTRODUCTION TO MECHANICS: FORCES AND KINEMATICS
Mechanics is the branch of mathematics that models the real-world behaviour of objects under the influence of forces and motion. This unit covers the fundamental concepts of forces in equilibrium and the kinematics of objects moving in a straight line.

FORCE: A push or pull acting on an object, measured in Newtons (N). Force is a vector quantity.
MASS: The amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms (kg). Mass is a scalar.

TYPES OF FORCES
Common forces encountered in AS Level Mechanics include:

- Weight (W): The gravitational force acting downward on an object. W = mg, where g ≈ 9.8 m/s² or 10 m/s².
- Normal Reaction (R or N): The perpendicular contact force exerted by a surface on an object.
- Tension (T): The pulling force in a string, rope, or rod.
- Friction (F): A force that opposes relative motion between surfaces in contact.
- Thrust: A pushing force, typically from a rod.

FRICTION
Friction acts to oppose the motion or tendency of motion of an object.

- Maximum (limiting) friction: F_max = μR, where μ is the coefficient of friction and R is the normal reaction.
- If the object is in equilibrium or moving at constant velocity, F ≤ μR.
- The friction force is only equal to μR when the object is on the point of sliding.

EQUILIBRIUM OF A PARTICLE
A particle is in equilibrium when the net force acting on it is zero.

- Resolve forces horizontally and vertically: ΣF_x = 0 and ΣF_y = 0.
- For objects on inclined planes, resolve parallel and perpendicular to the surface.

NOTE: Always draw a clear force diagram before resolving.

KINEMATICS: EQUATIONS OF MOTION
Kinematics describes the motion of objects without considering the cause. For uniform acceleration (constant a), five equations link the variables u, v, a, s, and t.

SUVAT EQUATIONS (for constant acceleration):
- v = u + at
- s = ut + 0.5at²
- s = vt - 0.5at²
- v² = u² + 2as
- s = 0.5(u + v)t

Where: u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, s = displacement, t = time.

VELOCITY–TIME GRAPHS
A velocity–time graph provides a powerful visual tool for analysing motion.

- The gradient of a v-t graph equals acceleration.
- The area under a v-t graph equals displacement.
- A horizontal line indicates constant velocity (zero acceleration).
- A straight sloped line indicates uniform (constant) acceleration.

VERTICAL MOTION UNDER GRAVITY
When an object moves vertically under gravity (neglecting air resistance), use the SUVAT equations with a = -g (taking upward as positive) or a = +g (taking downward as positive).

- At the highest point of a projectile's path, the vertical velocity v = 0.
- The time to reach the highest point equals the time to return to the same height.
