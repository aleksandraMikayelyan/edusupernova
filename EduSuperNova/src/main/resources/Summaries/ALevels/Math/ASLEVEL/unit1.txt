
QUADRATIC EXPRESSIONS AND FUNCTIONS
A quadratic expression is a polynomial of order 2. It has a maximum or minimum value, and its graph is a parabola with a characteristic curvature and symmetry.

QUADRATIC EXPRESSION: An expression of the form ax^2 + bx + c, where a, b, and c are real numbers and a ≠ 0.
LEADING COEFFICIENT: The coefficient 'a' of the x^2 term in a quadratic expression.
CONSTANT TERM: The term 'c' in a quadratic expression that does not contain the variable x.

FACTORIZING AND SOLVING
Factoring is the process of expressing a quadratic function as a product of two linear polynomials.

- Common methods include splitting the middle term or using the quadratic formula.
- Solving a quadratic equation ax^2 + bx + c = 0 involves finding the values of x (roots) where the expression equals zero[cite: 547].
- A quadratic equation can have zero, one (repeated), or two real roots.

COMPLETING THE SQUARE
Completing the square is a technique used to rewrite a quadratic expression in the form a(x + p)^2 + q.

NOTE: This form is particularly useful for identifying the coordinates of the vertex (turning point) of the parabola and for solving equations that do not factorize easily.

THE DISCRIMINANT
The discriminant allows you to determine the nature and number of roots of a quadratic equation without solving it.

DEFINITION: The discriminant is the value b^2 - 4ac from the quadratic formula.

- If b^2 - 4ac > 0: There are two distinct real roots.
- If b^2 - 4ac = 0: There is exactly one real root (a repeated root).
- If b^2 - 4ac < 0: There are no real roots.

SIMULTANEOUS EQUATIONS
To solve a pair of equations where one is linear and the other is quadratic, use the method of substitution.

1. Rearrange the linear equation to express one variable in terms of the other.
2. Substitute this expression into the quadratic equation.
3. Solve the resulting single-variable quadratic equation.
4. Substitute the found value(s) back into the linear equation to find the corresponding values for the second variable.

GRAPHS OF QUADRATIC FUNCTIONS
The graph of a quadratic function is a parabola. The relationship between the algebraic equation and the graph is defined by the coefficients.

- If a > 0: The parabola opens upwards (U-shape) and has a minimum point.
- If a < 0: The parabola opens downwards (n-shape) and has a maximum point.
- The x-intercepts of the graph correspond to the roots of the equation ax^2 + bx + c = 0.
- The y-intercept is always at the point (0, c).

KEY CONCEPT: The path of a projectile (trajectory) under gravity is modeled by a quadratic function, a discovery attributed to Galileo.
