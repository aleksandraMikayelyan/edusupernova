INTRODUCTION TO INTEGRATION
Integration is the reverse process of differentiation and is the second major branch of calculus. It is used to find areas under curves and to reconstruct original functions from their rates of change.

INTEGRATION: The process of finding the original function when its derivative is known. Also called anti-differentiation.

THE POWER RULE FOR INTEGRATION
The power rule is the fundamental rule for integrating polynomial terms.

- For any rational n ≠ -1: ∫ x^n dx = x^(n+1) / (n + 1) + c
- The constant c is the constant of integration and must always be included in indefinite integrals.
- A constant k integrates as: ∫ k dx = kx + c
- The sum/difference rule: the integral of a sum or difference is the sum or difference of individual integrals.

CONSTANT OF INTEGRATION
Every indefinite integral contains an arbitrary constant c, because differentiating any constant gives zero.

NOTE: The value of c is determined when a boundary condition is given — such as a known point on the curve.

DEFINITE INTEGRALS
A definite integral has specified upper and lower limits and produces a numerical value.

DEFINITION: ∫[a to b] f(x) dx = F(b) - F(a), where F(x) is any anti-derivative of f(x).

- The result represents the signed area between the curve y = f(x) and the x-axis from x = a to x = b.
- Regions below the x-axis give a negative result; regions above give a positive result.

FINDING THE EQUATION OF A CURVE
If the gradient function dy/dx is known and a point on the curve is given, the equation of the curve can be recovered by integrating.

Steps:
1. Integrate dy/dx to obtain y = F(x) + c.
2. Substitute the known point (x, y) to find c.
3. Write the complete equation of the curve.

AREA UNDER A CURVE
The area bounded by the curve y = f(x), the x-axis, and the vertical lines x = a and x = b is:

Area = ∫[a to b] f(x) dx, provided f(x) ≥ 0 on [a, b].

- If the curve dips below the x-axis, split the integral at the x-intercepts and take the absolute value of each part.

AREA BETWEEN TWO CURVES
The area enclosed between two curves y = f(x) and y = g(x) where f(x) ≥ g(x) is:

Area = ∫[a to b] [f(x) - g(x)] dx

where a and b are the x-coordinates of the intersection points.

NOTE: Always sketch the curves to determine which function lies above before setting up the integral.
