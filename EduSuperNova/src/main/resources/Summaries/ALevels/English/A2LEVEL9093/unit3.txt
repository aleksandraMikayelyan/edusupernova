DISCOURSE AND PRAGMATICS

PRAGMATICS
Pragmatics: The study of how context shapes meaning beyond the literal words spoken.
Utterance meaning vs sentence meaning: What is communicated vs what the words literally say.
Context: Physical setting, social roles, shared knowledge, previous discourse.
Deixis: Words that point to things in context (e.g. "here", "now", "this", "she").
  Personal deixis: I, you, he/she.
  Spatial deixis: here, there.
  Temporal deixis: now, then, yesterday.

SPEECH ACTS (Austin & Searle)
Locutionary act: The literal act of saying something.
Illocutionary act: The intended action performed through speech (e.g. promising, requesting, warning).
Perlocutionary act: The effect the utterance has on the listener.
Illocutionary force: The function or intention behind an utterance.
Performatives: Utterances that perform an action when spoken (e.g. "I promise", "I hereby sentence you").
Direct speech acts: Form matches function (e.g. "What time is it?" — genuine question).
Indirect speech acts: Form does not match function (e.g. "Can you pass the salt?" — a request, not a question about ability).

GRICE'S COOPERATIVE PRINCIPLE
Grice proposed that speakers follow maxims to communicate efficiently:
Maxim of Quantity: Be as informative as required; don't be more informative than necessary.
Maxim of Quality: Don't say what you believe to be false; don't say what you lack evidence for.
Maxim of Relation: Be relevant.
Maxim of Manner: Avoid obscurity and ambiguity; be brief and orderly.
Implicature: Meaning conveyed beyond the literal words, generated when maxims appear to be flouted.
  Conversational implicature: Context-dependent implied meaning.
  Conventional implicature: Meaning tied to specific words (e.g. "even", "but").
Flouting a maxim: Deliberately breaking a maxim to generate implicature (e.g. irony, understatement).

POLITENESS THEORY (Brown & Levinson)
Face: The public self-image that people want to maintain.
  Positive face: The desire to be liked, approved of, and accepted.
  Negative face: The desire to be free from imposition.
Face-Threatening Acts (FTAs): Utterances that threaten someone's positive or negative face.
Politeness strategies:
  Bald on record: Direct, no mitigation (e.g. "Sit down").
  Positive politeness: Affirm the listener's desires (e.g. "We're all in this together").
  Negative politeness: Minimise imposition (e.g. "I'm sorry to bother you, but...").
  Off record: Indirect, ambiguous (e.g. "It's cold in here" as a request to close a window).
  Withhold the FTA: Don't perform the act.

CONVERSATION ANALYSIS
Turn-taking: Rules governing when speakers change (Sacks, Schegloff & Jefferson).
  Transition Relevance Places (TRPs): Points where speaker change is possible.
  Adjacency pairs: Conversational sequences that come in pairs (e.g. question-answer, greeting-greeting).
  Preferred response: The expected, face-saving response.
  Dispreferred response: The unexpected response, often hedged or delayed.
Topic management: How topics are introduced, maintained, and changed.
Repairs: Corrections of errors or misunderstandings in conversation.
Backchannels: Signals that show a listener is paying attention (e.g. "mm", "yeah", "I see").
Interruptions vs overlaps: Interruptions violate turn-taking; overlaps are supportive.
