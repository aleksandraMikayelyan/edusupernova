NATIONAL INCOME AND THE MACROECONOMY

MEASURING NATIONAL INCOME
GDP (Gross Domestic Product): Total value of goods and services produced within a country in a year.
Three approaches (all should give the same result):
  Expenditure approach: GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)
  Output (production) approach: Sum of value added at each stage of production.
  Income approach: Sum of all factor incomes (wages + profits + rent + interest).
GNP (Gross National Product): GDP + net income from abroad.
NNP (Net National Product): GNP - depreciation (capital consumption).
GDP per capita: GDP divided by population; used to compare living standards.

LIMITATIONS OF GDP AS A WELFARE MEASURE
Does not capture: income distribution, non-market activity (e.g. household work), leisure, environmental damage.
Better alternatives: Human Development Index (HDI), Genuine Progress Indicator (GPI).

AGGREGATE DEMAND (AD)
AD = C + I + G + (X - M)
Consumption (C): Influenced by income, interest rates, wealth, expectations, taxes.
Investment (I): Influenced by interest rates, business confidence, technology, accelerator effect.
Government spending (G): Fiscal policy instrument.
Net exports (X - M): Influenced by exchange rates, foreign income, relative prices.
AD curve slopes downward: higher price level reduces real value of wealth, raises interest rates, reduces export competitiveness.

AGGREGATE SUPPLY (AS)
Short-run AS (SRAS): Slopes upward — higher prices increase profitability, encouraging more output.
Long-run AS (LRAS): Vertical at full employment — determined by productive capacity (land, labour, capital, enterprise).
Shifts of LRAS: Technology, education and training, immigration, capital investment.

MACROECONOMIC EQUILIBRIUM
Occurs where AD = AS.
Keynesian view: Economy can be stuck below full employment in short run; need for government intervention.
Classical view: Economy self-corrects to full employment in long run via flexible wages and prices.

THE MULTIPLIER
Initial increase in injections (I, G, or X) leads to a larger final increase in national income.
Multiplier (k) = 1 / (1 - MPC) = 1 / MPS + MPT + MPM
MPC: Marginal Propensity to Consume; MPS: to Save; MPT: to Tax; MPM: to Import.
Larger MPC → larger multiplier.

THE ACCELERATOR
Investment depends on the rate of change of national income.
Rising income → businesses invest to meet demand → further income growth.
Interaction of multiplier and accelerator → trade cycle (boom and bust).

MACROECONOMIC OBJECTIVES
Economic growth: Sustained increase in real GDP or GDP per capita.
Low unemployment: Target usually around 4-5% (natural rate of unemployment).
Low and stable inflation: Typically 2% target in many countries.
Balance of payments equilibrium: Sustainable current account position.
Equitable income distribution: Reducing inequality between groups.

TYPES OF UNEMPLOYMENT
Frictional: Short-term as workers move between jobs.
Structural: Mismatch between skills and available jobs (industry decline).
Cyclical (demand-deficient): Insufficient aggregate demand in the economy.
Seasonal: Demand fluctuates with seasons.
Real-wage: Wages held above equilibrium (e.g. by minimum wage or unions).
