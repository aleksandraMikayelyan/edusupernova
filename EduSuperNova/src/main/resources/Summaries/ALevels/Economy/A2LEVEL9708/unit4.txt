GOVERNMENT MACROECONOMIC POLICIES

FISCAL POLICY
Definition: Use of government spending and taxation to influence the economy.
Expansionary fiscal policy: Increase G or reduce T → higher AD → stimulates growth.
Contractionary fiscal policy: Reduce G or increase T → lower AD → controls inflation.

Budget positions:
  Budget deficit: G > T → government borrows (increases national debt).
  Budget surplus: T > G → government pays off debt.
  Balanced budget: T = G.
Automatic stabilisers: Tax and benefit systems that automatically stabilise the economy.
  Example: In recession, tax revenue falls and unemployment benefits rise → cushion the fall in AD.
Crowding out: Government borrowing pushes up interest rates → reduces private investment.
Time lags: Fiscal policy takes time to implement and take effect (recognition, decision, impact lags).

MONETARY POLICY
Definition: Use of interest rates, money supply, and credit conditions to influence the economy.
Tools:
  Interest rate changes: Central bank sets base rate; affects borrowing costs and consumption.
  Quantitative easing (QE): Central bank creates money to buy financial assets → increases money supply.
  Reserve requirements: Minimum fraction of deposits banks must hold.
Transmission mechanism: Interest rate cuts → lower borrowing costs → higher C and I → higher AD.
Limitations: Liquidity trap (interest rates at zero but no increase in spending), time lags.

SUPPLY-SIDE POLICIES
Aim to increase productive capacity (shift LRAS rightward).
Market-based supply-side policies:
  Tax cuts: Increase incentive to work and invest.
  Deregulation: Reduce barriers to competition and entry.
  Privatisation: Improve efficiency through profit motive.
  Trade union reform: Reduce wage rigidity and improve labour market flexibility.
Interventionist supply-side policies:
  Education and training: Improve human capital.
  Infrastructure investment: Reduce costs of production.
  Research and development: Encourage innovation.
  Industrial policy: Support strategic industries.

CONFLICTS BETWEEN MACROECONOMIC OBJECTIVES
Growth vs Inflation: Rapid growth can overheat economy and cause inflation (Keynesian).
Unemployment vs Inflation: Phillips Curve — inverse relationship in short run.
  Long-run Phillips Curve: Vertical at NAIRU — no permanent trade-off.
  NAIRU (Non-Accelerating Inflation Rate of Unemployment): Natural unemployment rate.
Growth vs Current account: Higher growth increases imports → worsens current account.
Inflation vs Competitiveness: High inflation raises export prices → reduces export demand.

EFFECTIVENESS OF POLICIES
Fiscal policy: Effective when there is spare capacity; less effective near full employment.
Monetary policy: Effective in controlling inflation; less so in recession (pushing on a string).
Supply-side policies: Long-term solutions but slow to take effect.
Policy mix: Combining policies to achieve multiple objectives simultaneously.

ECONOMIC INDICATORS
Leading indicators: Predict future economic activity (e.g. business confidence surveys).
Lagging indicators: Confirm trends after they occur (e.g. unemployment rate).
Coincident indicators: Move with the economy in real time (e.g. GDP growth).
