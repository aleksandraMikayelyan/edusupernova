THE PRICE SYSTEM IN THEORY AND PRACTICE

ELASTICITY OF DEMAND
Price Elasticity of Demand (PED): Measures the responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in price.
  PED = % change in quantity demanded / % change in price
  - PED > 1: Elastic demand (consumers sensitive to price changes)
  - PED < 1: Inelastic demand (consumers insensitive to price changes)
  - PED = 1: Unit elastic
Determinants of PED: availability of substitutes, proportion of income, necessity vs luxury, time period.

Income Elasticity of Demand (YED): Responsiveness of demand to a change in income.
  YED = % change in quantity demanded / % change in income
  - YED > 0: Normal good
  - YED < 0: Inferior good
  - YED > 1: Luxury good

Cross Elasticity of Demand (XED): Responsiveness of demand for one good to a price change in another.
  XED > 0: Substitutes
  XED < 0: Complements
  XED = 0: Unrelated goods

ELASTICITY OF SUPPLY
Price Elasticity of Supply (PES): Responsiveness of quantity supplied to a change in price.
  PES = % change in quantity supplied / % change in price
  - PES > 1: Elastic supply
  - PES < 1: Inelastic supply
Determinants: time period, factor mobility, spare capacity, storability.

CONSUMER AND PRODUCER SURPLUS
Consumer Surplus: The difference between what consumers are willing to pay and what they actually pay.
  Represented by the area above the price line and below the demand curve.
Producer Surplus: The difference between the price producers receive and the minimum they would accept.
  Represented by the area below the price line and above the supply curve.
Total (Community) Surplus: Consumer surplus + producer surplus = total welfare.

MARKET FAILURES
Externalities: Costs or benefits that fall on third parties not involved in the transaction.
  Negative externality (e.g. pollution): MSC > MPC → overproduction → deadweight loss.
  Positive externality (e.g. education): MSB > MPB → underproduction → deadweight loss.

Public Goods: Non-excludable and non-rivalrous (e.g. national defence, lighthouses).
  Free-rider problem: individuals consume without paying → market under-provides.

Merit Goods: Goods with greater social benefit than private benefit (e.g. healthcare, education).
  Government encourages consumption through subsidies or direct provision.

Demerit Goods: Goods with greater social cost than private cost (e.g. tobacco, alcohol).
  Government discourages consumption through taxes or regulation.

Information Failures: Asymmetric information leads to adverse selection and moral hazard.
  Example: Insurance markets, second-hand car markets (Akerlof's "lemons" problem).

GOVERNMENT POLICIES TO CORRECT MARKET FAILURES
Taxes: Internalise negative externalities by raising price to reflect MSC (Pigouvian tax).
Subsidies: Encourage consumption/production of goods with positive externalities.
Price controls: Maximum prices (e.g. rent control) and minimum prices (e.g. minimum wage).
Regulation: Direct controls on quantity, quality, or behaviour.
Tradeable permits: Cap-and-trade systems for pollution rights.
Provision of information: Reduce information asymmetry.
