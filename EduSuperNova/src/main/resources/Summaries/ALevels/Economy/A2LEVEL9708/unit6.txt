DEVELOPMENT ECONOMICS

ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT VS ECONOMIC GROWTH
Economic growth: Increase in real GDP or GDP per capita over time.
Economic development: Broader concept including improvements in living standards, health, education, freedom, equality, and sustainability.
Sustainable development: Growth that meets present needs without compromising future generations.

CHARACTERISTICS OF DEVELOPING ECONOMIES
Low GDP per capita and low incomes.
High proportion of population in agriculture.
Rapid population growth; high dependency ratio.
Poor infrastructure (roads, electricity, internet).
Limited access to healthcare and education → low HDI.
High levels of absolute poverty and income inequality.
Dependence on primary commodity exports → vulnerable to price fluctuations.
Large informal sector; limited financial markets.

MEASURING DEVELOPMENT
Human Development Index (HDI): Composite of life expectancy, education (mean and expected years of schooling), and GNI per capita.
  HDI close to 1 = high development; close to 0 = low development.
Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI): Measures poverty across health, education, and living standards.
Gini coefficient: Measures income inequality; 0 = perfect equality; 1 = perfect inequality.
Lorenz Curve: Graphical representation of income distribution.
Absolute poverty: Living below a minimum standard (e.g. under $2.15/day, World Bank threshold).
Relative poverty: Living below a defined proportion of average income in a society.

BARRIERS TO DEVELOPMENT
Primary product dependency: Volatile prices, declining terms of trade (Prebisch-Singer hypothesis).
Capital flight: Wealthy individuals move assets overseas → less domestic investment.
Debt burden: High external debt repayments reduce government spending on development.
Political instability: Discourages investment and disrupts economic activity.
Brain drain: Skilled workers emigrate → loss of human capital.
Poor governance: Corruption diverts resources from development.
Population growth: Rapid growth reduces GDP per capita and strains public services.

STRATEGIES FOR DEVELOPMENT
Industrialisation: Shift from agriculture to manufacturing (Lewis model: labour moves from low-productivity agriculture to higher-productivity industry).
Import Substitution Industrialisation (ISI): Protect domestic industries behind tariffs to develop own manufacturing base.
Export-led growth: Use comparative advantage to expand exports (East Asian Tigers model).
Microfinance: Small loans to entrepreneurs in developing countries.
Foreign Direct Investment (FDI): Brings capital, technology, and jobs; risk of profit repatriation.
Official Development Assistance (ODA / Aid):
  Bilateral aid: Government to government.
  Multilateral aid: Through international organisations (IMF, World Bank, UN agencies).
  Arguments for: Fills savings gap, funds infrastructure, provides expertise.
  Arguments against: Creates dependency, may be tied to donor conditions, corruption.
Debt relief: HIPC initiative — cancellation of unpayable debts of heavily indebted poor countries.
Trade liberalisation: Giving developing countries access to rich country markets.
Institutional reform: Property rights, rule of law, transparent governance.

ROLE OF INTERNATIONAL INSTITUTIONS
IMF (International Monetary Fund): Provides short-term balance of payments support; structural adjustment conditions.
World Bank: Long-term development loans for infrastructure and poverty reduction.
WTO: Oversees trade rules; developing countries argue rules favour rich nations.
NGOs: Non-governmental organisations provide targeted local development assistance.
