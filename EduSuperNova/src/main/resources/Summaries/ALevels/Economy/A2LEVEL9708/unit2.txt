GOVERNMENT MICROECONOMIC INTERVENTION

TAXATION
Direct taxes: Levied on income and profit (e.g. income tax, corporation tax).
  Progressive tax: Higher earners pay a higher percentage rate.
  Regressive tax: Takes a larger proportion of income from lower earners.
Indirect taxes: Levied on expenditure (e.g. VAT, excise duty).
  Specific tax: Fixed amount per unit (shifts supply curve upward by constant amount).
  Ad valorem tax: Percentage of price (shifts supply curve upward by increasing amount).
Tax incidence: Who bears the burden depends on relative elasticity of demand and supply.
  Inelastic demand → consumers bear more of the tax burden.
  Elastic demand → producers bear more of the tax burden.

SUBSIDIES
A payment by government to producers or consumers to lower price or increase output.
Effect: Shifts supply curve rightward, reducing equilibrium price and increasing quantity.
Subsidy incidence: Who benefits depends on elasticity; inelastic supply → producers benefit more.
Used to encourage positive externalities, support merit goods, or assist specific industries.

PRICE CONTROLS
Maximum price (price ceiling): Set below equilibrium to make goods affordable.
  Creates excess demand (shortage) as quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied.
  Examples: rent controls, food price caps.
Minimum price (price floor): Set above equilibrium to protect producers.
  Creates excess supply (surplus) as quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded.
  Examples: minimum wage, EU Common Agricultural Policy (CAP) intervention prices.

REGULATION
Direct controls on market behaviour imposed by government or regulatory bodies.
Types: pollution limits, product safety standards, competition law, licensing.
Regulatory capture: Regulated industries influence regulators in their own favour.
Cost-benefit analysis (CBA): Used to evaluate public projects by comparing total costs and benefits.
  Challenge: Placing monetary values on intangible costs/benefits (e.g. lives, environment).

COMPETITION POLICY
Aims to promote competition and prevent monopoly abuse.
Anti-competitive behaviour: Price-fixing, market sharing, predatory pricing, abuse of dominance.
Merger control: Authorities assess whether mergers substantially reduce competition.
Natural monopoly: Declining average costs throughout market → single firm most efficient.
  Regulated to prevent exploitation: price caps (RPI-X), rate of return regulation.

PRIVATISATION AND DEREGULATION
Privatisation: Transfer of state-owned enterprises to private ownership.
  Arguments for: efficiency, profit motive, reduced government borrowing.
  Arguments against: profit over public interest, natural monopoly exploitation.
Deregulation: Removal of legal barriers to entry in markets.
  Encourages competition but may reduce safety standards.
Nationalisation: Government takes control of private enterprises.
  Justified for natural monopolies, strategic industries, or market failure correction.

LABOUR MARKET ISSUES
Wage determination: Interaction of labour demand (MRP) and labour supply.
Monopsony: Single buyer of labour → wages driven below competitive level.
Trade unions: Collective bargaining raises wages but may reduce employment.
Wage differentials: Explained by skill levels, compensating differentials, discrimination.
Minimum wage: Reduces poverty but may cause unemployment if set above equilibrium.
