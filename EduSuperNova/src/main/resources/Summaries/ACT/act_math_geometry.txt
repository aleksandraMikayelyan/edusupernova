GEOMETRY

ANGLES:

ANGLE RELATIONSHIPS:
- Complementary angles: sum to 90°
- Supplementary angles: sum to 180°
- Vertical angles (formed by intersecting lines): always equal
- Linear pair: adjacent angles on a straight line, sum to 180°

PARALLEL LINES CUT BY A TRANSVERSAL:
- Corresponding angles: equal (same position at each intersection)
- Alternate interior angles: equal (between the parallel lines, opposite sides of transversal)
- Alternate exterior angles: equal (outside the parallel lines, opposite sides of transversal)
- Co-interior (same-side interior / consecutive interior) angles: supplementary (sum to 180°)

KEY CONCEPT: If you see parallel lines on the ACT, immediately think about these angle relationships. Most problems resolve by identifying equal angle pairs.

TRIANGLES:

ANGLE SUM: All three interior angles of a triangle sum to 180°.

EXTERIOR ANGLE THEOREM: An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles.

TRIANGLE TYPES:
- Equilateral: all sides equal, all angles = 60°
- Isosceles: two sides equal, two base angles equal
- Scalene: no sides equal, no angles equal
- Right triangle: one angle = 90°; the side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse
- Obtuse: one angle > 90°
- Acute: all angles < 90°

TRIANGLE INEQUALITY: The sum of any two sides must be greater than the third side.

AREA OF A TRIANGLE: A = ½ × base × height
- The height must be PERPENDICULAR to the base (it's not always a side of the triangle)

PYTHAGOREAN THEOREM: For right triangles: a² + b² = c² where c is the hypotenuse.

PYTHAGOREAN TRIPLES (memorize these):
- 3-4-5 and multiples: 6-8-10, 9-12-15, 5-12-13
- 8-15-17
- 7-24-25

SPECIAL RIGHT TRIANGLES:

45-45-90 TRIANGLE: legs are equal; hypotenuse = leg × √2
- If leg = 1, hypotenuse = √2
- If hypotenuse = 10, leg = 10/√2 = 5√2

30-60-90 TRIANGLE: sides in ratio 1 : √3 : 2
- Short leg (opposite 30°) : Long leg (opposite 60°) : Hypotenuse = 1 : √3 : 2
- If short leg = 5, long leg = 5√3, hypotenuse = 10

IMPORTANT: Identify special right triangles immediately — they save time on the ACT.

SIMILAR TRIANGLES: Same shape, different size. Corresponding angles are equal; corresponding sides are proportional.
- If two triangles are similar with ratio k:1, their areas are in ratio k²:1

CONGRUENT TRIANGLES: Exactly the same shape and size.
Congruence shortcuts: SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS (note: SSA and AAA do NOT guarantee congruence)

QUADRILATERALS:

PROPERTIES:
- PARALLELOGRAM: opposite sides parallel and equal; opposite angles equal; diagonals bisect each other. Area = base × height.
- RECTANGLE: parallelogram with all right angles. Area = length × width; diagonal = √(l² + w²).
- RHOMBUS: parallelogram with all sides equal; diagonals bisect each other at right angles. Area = ½ × d₁ × d₂ (where d₁ and d₂ are the diagonals).
- SQUARE: rectangle AND rhombus. All properties of both. Area = s²; diagonal = s√2.
- TRAPEZOID: exactly one pair of parallel sides. Area = ½ × (b₁ + b₂) × h.

ANGLE SUM: The interior angles of any quadrilateral sum to 360°.

CIRCLES:

KEY TERMS:
- RADIUS (r): distance from center to any point on circle
- DIAMETER (d): d = 2r; longest chord
- CHORD: line segment with both endpoints on circle
- SECANT: line that intersects circle at two points
- TANGENT: line that touches circle at exactly one point; perpendicular to radius at that point
- ARC: portion of circle's circumference

FORMULAS:
- Circumference: C = 2πr = πd
- Area: A = πr²

ARC LENGTH: (central angle / 360°) × 2πr
SECTOR AREA: (central angle / 360°) × πr²

INSCRIBED ANGLE THEOREM: An inscribed angle (vertex ON the circle) = ½ × intercepted arc = ½ × central angle.

KEY CONCEPT: An inscribed angle that intercepts a semicircle (diameter as chord) = 90°.

TANGENT LINE: Always perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency. If a tangent and a radius meet at a point on the circle, they form a right angle — use Pythagorean theorem.

COORDINATE GEOMETRY:

MIDPOINT FORMULA: Midpoint = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)

DISTANCE FORMULA: d = √[(x₂-x₁)² + (y₂-y₁)²] (derived from Pythagorean theorem)

SLOPE: m = (y₂-y₁)/(x₂-x₁) = rise/run
- Positive slope: rises left to right
- Negative slope: falls left to right
- Zero slope: horizontal line
- Undefined slope: vertical line

PARALLEL LINES: equal slopes (m₁ = m₂)
PERPENDICULAR LINES: slopes are negative reciprocals (m₁ × m₂ = -1)

SLOPE-INTERCEPT FORM: y = mx + b (m = slope, b = y-intercept)
STANDARD FORM: Ax + By = C

EQUATION OF A CIRCLE: (x - h)² + (y - k)² = r² where (h, k) is the center and r is the radius.

THREE-DIMENSIONAL FIGURES:

RECTANGULAR PRISM (box):
- Volume = l × w × h
- Surface Area = 2(lw + lh + wh)
- Space diagonal = √(l² + w² + h²)

CYLINDER:
- Volume = πr²h
- Surface Area = 2πr² + 2πrh (two circles + lateral surface)

CONE:
- Volume = ⅓πr²h
- Slant height l = √(r² + h²)

SPHERE:
- Volume = (4/3)πr³
- Surface Area = 4πr²

CUBE:
- Volume = s³; Surface Area = 6s²; Space diagonal = s√3

IMPORTANT: The ACT often asks for volume or surface area — know whether it's asking for ONE or the OTHER and don't confuse the formulas.

REMEMBER: Always mark up your diagrams. Label angles, sides, and known values directly on the figure. Looking for special triangles, parallel lines, or right angles can unlock many geometry problems instantly.
